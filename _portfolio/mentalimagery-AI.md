## Abstract
Current AI does not have visual mental imagery. However, there is a growing body of research investigating the possibility. This may be due to, on the one hand, traditional cognitive science and the mental imagery debate, where the goal was to programme mental imagery in AI. On the other hand, this may also be due to a separate literature on our increasing beliefs about AI capacities. It remains, however, unknown whether we intuitively believe that AI might have mental imagery or not. Therefore, the goal of this research programme is to investigate what beliefs on mental imagery people attribute to AI. This can be operationalised in either participants viewing AI-related static scenarios; participants watching AI performing mental imagery tasks; or even participants interacting with AI - all of which to be followed by asking participants on their interpretations.

## Why this work matters?
Mental imagery plays a role in other higher-level functions, such as memory or planning, therefore it has a role in intelligence as well. Moreover, AI is increasingly used in mental health advice. Whether AI would benefit from mental imagery capabilities - in terms of its intelligence or in mental health - therefore becomes an important future direction.

## Open questions remaining
* Do our AI-related mental imagery beliefs depend on how we define mental imagery?
* What are the criteria people use to have such beliefs?
* What such beliefs do we have about humanoid robots?

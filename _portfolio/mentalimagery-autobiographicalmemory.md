## Abstract
Autobiographical memories – the memories of personal life events – are linked to vivid visual mental imagery. These memories can be retrieved involuntarily, voluntarily with no effort, or voluntarily with effort. Previous research shows that vividness ratings are lower for voluntary and effortful retrieval, compared to involuntary or voluntary but effortless memories. Experimental paradigms range from involving stimuli such as words with different levels of imageability; to measuring spontaneously occurring memories during a monotonic task; or even pairing pictures with sounds during encoding and subsequently presenting only the sounds to participants. However, it remains unclear to what extent such experimental paradigms of autobiographical memory rely on visual mental imagery. Furthermore, it remains to be determined how those with aphantasia perform on these tasks. Therefore, a replication of these experimental paradigms are warranted, with manipulations of task instructions, provided that task instructions tap into different cognitive processes.

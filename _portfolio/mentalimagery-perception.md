## Abstract
Villena-González et al. (2016) presented an experimental paradigm, in which it was demonstrated that when participants were paying attention to their internal thoughts, their EEG signals in occipital areas responded less to external stimuli, as opposed to when they were paying attention externally. Moreover, paying attention to visual mental images, as opposed to inner speech, had a stronger effect on these EEG signals. This is then interpreted as visual mental imagery taking away more neural resources from visual perception in occipital areas than inner speech, indicating shared neural correlates; one of the arguments of the depictive view in the mental imagery debate. However, several concerns were not taken into account in this design. Vividness of mental imagery was not measured; stimuli were not specified (what participants should focus on inwardly); and no manipulation checks were included to verify adherence to task instructions. Accommodating these concerns into the experimental paradigm, therefore, becomes the goal of this research programme. This involves employing trial-level vividness ratings, setting up predefined sets of visual stimuli, and additional instruction manipulations with a corresponding manipulation check.

## Why this work matters?
This unique research design goes back to an earlier topic, whether neuroscience can inform the nature of mental images. The study of Villena-González et al. (2016) - in my opinion - was overlooked as there are no indications of direct or conceptual replications of it in the literature. Given the inherent challenges of interpreting EEG signals, the paradigm requires further scrutiny.

## Abstract
Concrete but uncertain situations (e.g., hearing an unfamiliar sound during the night) can involve mental imagery and emotional responses (e.g., fear). In addition, psychophysiological reactions (e.g., pupil dilation) can occur in relation to both mental imagery and emotion. However, it is unclear to what extent these processes and responses drive decision making in uncertainty – whether engagement with the situation occurs or not –, and how these processes unfold in people with aphantasia. Therefore, the goal of this research programme is to study mental imagery and emotional responses in decision making under uncertainty, in addition to measuring pupil dilation. The predictive processing theory of the brain provides a general framework, in which an internal model is constantly compared and corrected to the external world. Mental imagery could be understood as part of this internal model. Operationalisation follows decision making paradigms. For example, participants could read emotionally valenced or high-imageability (versus low) scenarios, followed by a decision to make. Pupillary responses are simultaneously recorded, while subjective reports can be registered after each scenario (e.g., vividness ratings).

## Why this work matters?
Mental imagery in emotional processing is extensively studied in clinical psychology. People with emotional disorders, such as social anxiety or post-traumatic stress disorder, tend to avoid situations (i.e., not taking a decision), which evoke negative imagery or emotions. Understanding the relationship between these factors thus becomes relevant, especially when one considers the possibility of no mental imagery involved (i.e., aphantasia).
